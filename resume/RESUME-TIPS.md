# How to Get Employers to Pick You — 2026 Playbook

Research-backed notes behind the redesign of your resume. Sources are linked at the bottom.

---

## The 30-second reality of 2026 hiring

- **~75% of resumes are filtered out by software before a human ever reads them.** Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) are used on ~98% of Fortune 500 career sites. Your resume has to be machine-readable *first*, impressive *second*.
- **Recruiters spend ~7 seconds** on the first human pass. They skim the summary and the first bullet under each job. Put your strongest, most relevant proof there.
- **82% of employers now prioritize skills over degrees.** Google, Apple, and IBM dropped degree requirements for many roles. Not having a CS degree is no longer the disqualifier it used to be — *if* you show real skills and shipped work.

**Takeaway for you:** Your founder story (Design over Atlanta, 150+ projects, 5+ years) and your live portfolio *are* your credential. Lead with them.

---

## 1. Make it ATS-safe (this is non-negotiable)

What the new resume does, and why:

| Do this | Why |
|---|---|
| Single-column layout | Multi-column resumes get scrambled when the parser reads them left-to-right. |
| Standard section headings ("Professional Summary", "Experience", "Skills", "Education") | Parsers look for exact, conventional labels to sort your content. Creative headings ("Where I've Made Magic") get dropped. |
| Real, selectable text — no text inside images | A parser can't read text baked into a graphic or logo. |
| No tables, icons, skill-bars, or photos for content | These trip up parsers and add nothing a recruiter scores. |
| Standard fonts (Helvetica/Arial/Calibri) | Decorative fonts can fail to map characters correctly. |
| Save as a **text-based PDF** (or DOCX) | The redesign exports to a clean, selectable PDF. Avoid "image" PDFs from scanners or design tools. |

> **Test it:** copy-paste your finished PDF into a plain text editor. If everything comes out in the right order and nothing is missing, an ATS can read it. If it's jumbled, fix the layout.

## 2. Mirror the job posting's language

ATS in 2026 weighs **evidence over keyword-stuffing** — the tools you actually used, the scale of your work, and outcomes that match the role. But you still need the *employer's wording*.

- For each application, open the job posting and list its core requirements.
- Where you genuinely have that experience, use **their phrasing** ("React", "REST APIs", "customer support", "B2B sales") in your summary and skills.
- Aim to cover the posting's must-haves. A 65–75%+ keyword match is the target most ATS tools recommend.
- **Never invent skills to match.** If it's not true, leave it out.

This is why the resume is built to be *tailored per application*, not sent identically everywhere.

---

## 3. Every bullet: action verb + what you did + measurable result

The 2026 standard is the **XYZ formula**: *Accomplished **X**, measured by **Y**, by doing **Z**.* Task descriptions ("responsible for…") no longer make the cut.

**Weak (before):**
> Created automated data scraping solutions

**Strong (after — used in your resume):**
> Built automated data-scraping and data-entry tools that **cut clients' manual data work by up to 80%**.

Start each bullet with a strong verb: *Built, Designed, Shipped, Automated, Reduced, Grew, Launched, Developed.* Avoid "responsible for", "helped with", "worked on".

### Numbers to plug in (your homework)
Your resume is strongest when the metrics are *yours*. Swap in real figures wherever you can:
- How many clients/projects per year? Average turnaround?
- A specific automation: hours saved per week? error rate before/after?
- Revenue or savings you created for a client (even one strong case study).
- Hosting savings ("$3/mo vs. $20+/mo") if you want a cost-comparison bullet.

Placeholders like "up to 80%" and "150+" are pulled from your existing materials — confirm they're accurate before sending.

## 4. Lead with the strongest version of your story

- **Put Design over Atlanta first.** A founder/full-stack role outranks the service jobs for any tech employer. The redesign does this.
- **Group the service jobs** (lifeguard, McDonald's, Domino's, detailing) into one compact "Additional Experience" line. They show reliability and work ethic without crowding out your developer work. Don't delete them — gaps raise questions — just don't let them dominate.
- **Fold sales/IT-support skills into the developer narrative.** Your B2B sales and tech-support background is a genuine differentiator: most developers can't sell or talk to clients. The resume frames it as why you win and keep clients.

---

## 5. The summary is your 7-second pitch

Use the **title-led formula**: *[Adjective] [Job Title] with [N] years in [domain], who [signature proof point].*

Yours reads: *"Self-taught full-stack developer with 5+ years building custom web applications… for 150+ small businesses…"* — role, years, scale, and proof in the first sentence, exactly where recruiters look.

---

## 6. Things that quietly help you get picked

- **One page if you can, two max.** You're comfortably one page.
- **Link your portfolio and GitHub.** For a self-taught dev, the portfolio *is* the proof. Your site (designoveratlanta.com) is already linked — add a GitHub link if you have public repos.
- **Add a LinkedIn URL** and keep the profile consistent with the resume (recruiters cross-check).
- **Consider one free/low-cost certificate** (e.g., a cloud or front-end cert) — for a no-degree candidate it signals commitment and adds keywords.
- **Proofread ruthlessly.** A single typo is one of the fastest ways to get cut. Read it aloud.
- **Filename matters:** save as `Hunter-Weeks-Resume.pdf`, not `resume-final-v3.pdf`.
- **Match the resume to the role you want.** If you target IT-support roles instead of dev roles, reorder skills/experience to lead with support and troubleshooting.
